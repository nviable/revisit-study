Study title: Structured Expert Evaluation of STOP&SCAN, a Framework for Evaluating Potentially Synthetic Media

Principal Investigator: Saniat Javid Sohrawardi — john.sohrawardi@rit.edu
Co-Investigator: Kelly Wu, Rochester Institute of Technology
Collaborating researcher: Fatma Aksu, University of Bologna — fatma.aksu2@unibo.it

You are being asked to take part in a research study. This document explains what the study involves so you can decide whether you want to participate. Please read it carefully, and email the Principal Investigator with any questions before you decide. Taking part is entirely voluntary.

Why you are being asked

You are being invited because of your professional work in digital media forensics, misinformation research, media literacy education, or fact-checking and verification journalism. We are seeking the judgment of about 10–15 professionals across these four areas.

What this study is about

We have developed a framework called STOP&SCAN, intended to help non-experts evaluate images, video, and audio that may be AI-generated or misleading. It is a short reasoning sequence: a pause to register your initial reaction (STOP), followed by four steps: Source, Content, Alignment, and Now Reflect.

The framework is designed around a specific idea: that dismissing authentic content is as serious an error as believing fabricated content. We have derived it from research literature and tested it on paper against documented cases. We have not yet had it evaluated by people who do this work professionally, which is what we are asking you to do.

We want your criticism. If the framework is unclear, impractical, or wrong, that is the most useful result you can give us, and we would rather hear it now than after publication.

What you will be asked to do

The study is a single online session of about 55 to 60 minutes. You can take part at whatever time and place suits you. Nobody will observe you, and there is no scheduled appointment.

You will:

  • Answer a few short questions about your professional background.
  • Read four evaluations our team carried out on four real, publicly documented cases, one step at a time, and judge the reasoning.
  • After each case, answer questions about what would have happened had someone done less.
  • Read short alternative SIFT routes on two of the cases and tell us whether we represented them fairly.
  • Rate both frameworks and answer open questions about where each would fail.

About the case material. The four cases are drawn from public sources through the AI Incident Database and published Snopes fact-checks. Two involve photographs of public figures in politically contested circumstances. One involves a fraud in which a person lost their savings to a cloned voice. One involves a government agency publishing an erroneous graphic. None contains graphic, violent, or sexual content.

About the evaluations. They are written deliberately in plain terms, as a capable non-expert following the sequence would reason. They are demonstrations rather than authoritative analyses, and one of the four does not reach a conclusion. That is not an error in the material.

About how the instrument behaves

  • The steps unlock one at a time. Each step opens only after you submit the one before it. Once opened, steps stay visible.
  • Submitted responses cannot be edited. You will still see what you wrote, but you will not be able to change it.

What we record: your written responses, ratings, background categories, which steps and reference cards you opened, and how long you spent on each. We do not record your keystrokes, your screen, audio, or video, and we do not record your IP address.

Is there any payment?

No. There is no payment, gift card, or other compensation. If you would find it useful, we will send you a summary of what we find.

Risks

  • Being identified. The professional community is small. We collect experience as a broad range, do not ask where you work, describe participants only by one of four broad fields, and screen quotations. We cannot promise this risk is zero. If there is anything you write that you would prefer we never quote, tell us and we will not quote it.
  • Professional discomfort. There are no right answers, we are not scoring you, and no individual’s responses will be reported. Critical responses are what we are hoping for.
  • The material itself. Two cases involve politically contested content about named public figures, and one involves fraud. You can skip any question and stop at any point.

Benefits

We cannot promise you any direct benefit. You may find it professionally interesting, and you may request a summary of the findings. The broader purpose is to avoid putting flawed guidance in front of the public.

Your right to stop

Participation is entirely voluntary. You may skip any question. You may stop at any time by closing the browser. If you stop part-way and would like prior responses deleted, email the Principal Investigator. We can do this until we have finished analysis and removed the identity link.

How your information is protected

Your responses are stored under a random participant ID, not your name. Your name and email are kept in a separate file that only the Principal Investigator and the Co-Investigator responsible for recruitment can open. Data are stored on RIT-managed, encrypted, MFA-protected institutional storage. No commercial AI system outside RIT processes your responses. De-identified data may be shared with a collaborating researcher at the University of Bologna and may be published or deposited after screening.

For participants in Europe (EEA, UK, or Switzerland)

Controller: Rochester Institute of Technology. Lawful basis: your explicit consent. International transfer to the United States takes place on the basis of your explicit consent. You may request access, correction, deletion, or restriction, and you may withdraw consent at any time by emailing the Principal Investigator. You may lodge a complaint with your national data protection authority.
